# 022 — The Tenant Downstairs
Mode: wild (monsters under every building, silly-spooky; all-ages: adult narrator, rent, a crooked landlord) — opener: in-this-world (pick_mode). Hook = line 33 (the beast's blazing eyes over Dana, then the giant claw coming down at her).
Angle: secret-helper — something downstairs has been paying Dana's rent all year; the twist is who (the monster everyone is told never to disturb) and why (the plate of leftovers she leaves by the basement door "for the rats").
Premise: In this world, every building comes with a monster. It lives in the basement, it's in the lease, and you keep it happy — or it keeps you up all night.
Logline: Dana is three months behind on rent, yet every month a receipt marked "Paid in full" slides under her door — and when her greedy landlord breaks into the basement with a shovel to find the gold, she follows him down and meets the tenant downstairs: a building-sized beast in reading glasses who has been paying her rent out of its own hoard, kept every plate she ever left by its door, and turns out to own the place.
Twist / surprising beats: the giant claw that comes down "right at me" hands her a receipt; the monster puts on reading glasses and opens a ledger; the shelf of hundreds of washed plates ("I was never feeding the rats"); the lease's last page — OWNER: THE TENANT DOWNSTAIRS (it built No. 9 four hundred years ago, Mr. Crane only collected the rent and kept it); it writes him a one-word notice; rent is now due on Fridays, in lasagna.
Gags: the opera house's monster wants a lullaby every night "and it has notes" (score card: PITCHY, 4/10 — callback at the end: dinner and a show, 10/10); the laundromat's monster takes one sock from every load ("now you know").
