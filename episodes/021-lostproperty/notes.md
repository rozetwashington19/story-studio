# 021 — Lost Property
Mode: heartfelt (a grand railway station; all-ages: adult narrator, estranged siblings) — opener: cold-open (pick_mode). Hook = line 40 in slow motion (Mara mid-leap for the moving Night Train).
Angle: race-against-time — the Night Train leaves at six and takes everything unclaimed; a clock is in almost every shot (5:40 → 6:00).
Premise: Everything the city loses ends up at the station's Lost Property counter by evening. Only the owner can pick it up — and whatever isn't claimed by six leaves on the Night Train for good.
Logline: Mara has run the Lost Property counter for eleven years and never lost a thing — except her brother — until, at ten to six, a soggy envelope in his handwriting rolls in that only he can claim, and she chases it onto the Night Train to find he's been driving it, and losing that same letter, every day for four years.
Twist / surprising beats: nobody but the owner can lift a lost thing (Mara can't budge a key ring, or the envelope); the Night Train's driver IS the brother; the mail car is stacked floor to ceiling with four years of the same two-word letter; she "claims" him with a luggage tag; the lost hour nobody claims (gag 3) is the hour the train leaves late.
Gags: Mr. Abel lost his keys, then his temper (a furious little red monster under a glass dome — it bites); the mayor lost her train of thought (a toy train hauling thought-clouds round platform two); somebody lost a whole hour (a glowing hourglass nobody claims).
